### WR-01: Info document prints chamfer rows and a volume for angles the builder refuses

**Files modified:** `src/screw/calc/__init__.py`, `src/screw/calc/thread.py`,
`src/screw/solid/__init__.py`, `tests/test_calc.py`, `tests/test_api.py`, `tests/test_solid.py`,
`docs/tech_debt/INDEX.md`, `docs/tech_debt/active/2026-10-24-info-document-cannot-see-a-kernel-refused-head-chamfer.md`
**Commit:** ea3363a
**Status:** fixed: requires human verification (logic change in what `derive()` withholds)
**Applied fix:**
- `_bolt` asks `head_chamfer_volume` the same question the builder does. When it raises, the four
  chamfer rows and the volume are left out and one warning says the part is not built. The band
  warning ("built as asked") is not emitted for that part, and the false "not a finite number"
  volume warning no longer occurs. The API, the CLI and the page read `derive()` and follow.
- `_build_bolt` catches the oracle's `ValueError` and raises `BuildError("Head chamfer refused
  before the geometry kernel ran: ...; raise head_chamfer or set it to 0.")`, so the text no
  longer says "Geometry kernel failed" for a kernel that never ran.
- The oracle's message prints the angle with `repr`, so 5e-324 reads 5e-324 and not 4.94066e-324.
- Tests: the old 5e-324 volume-warning test is replaced by one that pins no chamfer rows, no
  volume row, the not-built warning, and the absence of "built as asked" and "not a finite
  number". A control pins 1e-4, which builds, keeping its rows and volume. The API test checks
  the info document and that the 422 message does not say "Geometry kernel failed". The solid
  test checks the same message for 5e-324.
- No angle floor (D-18).

**Deviation from the instruction (surfaced, not silently approximated):** the owner asked for
1e-6 to be pinned as refused, with no chamfer rows. It cannot be, without a floor. The oracle
accepts 1e-6 (and every angle from 1e-320 up). The kernel refuses it (`Standard_Failure`), and
`derive()` never runs the kernel. I measured it: the oracle raises only at 5e-324. Angles
1e-320 to 1e-5 are oracle-accepted and kernel-refused; 1e-4 and above build. So 1e-6 still prints
its rows and a volume. I filed this as a tech-debt item (severity must) rather than add a floor:
`docs/tech_debt/active/2026-10-24-info-document-cannot-see-a-kernel-refused-head-chamfer.md`,
with an INDEX row. Its next step is a linux measurement, then either a "may be refused" warning
(not a floor) or a superseding decision.

**Residual fixed (2026-10-24, owner ruling: warn below the measured limit, no floor)**, commit
c0e6d8f. The tech-debt item is resolved (`docs/tech_debt/resolved/`). `derive()` now withholds the
four chamfer rows and the volume for `0 < head_chamfer < head_chamfer_kernel_bound(s, k)` and
warns once that the kernel may refuse the part. D-18 stands: no model floor, no 422 change.
- The refusal is not one angle. Smallest angle that built, through `solid.build`, on macOS arm64 and
  on the project image as linux/amd64 (emulated), the same cadquery-ocp 7.9.3.1.1: 1e-4 for the
  default (s 10, k 4) and M2 (s 4, k 1.4) heads on both; 5e-5 (host) and 2e-5 (linux) for M20;
  5e-5 for s 100; 2e-4, 5e-4, 1e-3, 2e-3 for s 2, 1, 0.5, 0.25; 5e-4 and 5e-3 for s 10 with k 20
  and k 1000 (host). It refuses with the piece gate ("wrong head chamfer") and, at 1e-6 and
  below on small heads, `Standard_Failure`. The two platforms agree on every cell but M20 at 2e-5.
- The gate's own tolerance does not predict it: T_PIECE against the volume integral's bias
  (5.4e-11 * s^2 * k mm3) gives 5.5e-5 * k / s degrees, 3x to 10x below the refusals, which come
  from a sliver the kernel does not cut. So the bound is fitted: `max(2e-4, 3.5e-3 * k / s^2)`.
- Confirmed on a fresh grid, both platforms: the 15 grade A ISO 4014 heads and 20 off-catalogue
  heads (s 0.3 to 50, k/s 0.4 to 40) at the bound, 1.01x, 2x, 10x, 1 and 30 degrees: 210 cells per
  platform, all built. Half the bound builds for every catalogue head; a quarter does not for M2
  to M7 and M14.
- The 1e-6 case the owner first asked to pin is now covered, by a warning rather than a floor.

### WR-02: `bolt.assemble(..., head=None)` silently builds the sharp head

**Files modified:** `src/screw/solid/bolt.py`, `bench/head_fuse.py`, `bench/end_cuts.py`,
`tests/test_solid.py`
**Commit:** caf4245
**Applied fix:**
- `head` is a required argument of `bolt.assemble`; mypy `--strict` names every caller.
- `bench/head_fuse.py` gets `product_head(p)` (the builder's chamfer cut without its piece gate)
  and `head_chamfer_term(p)`. `assemble`, `closed` and the head+shank bytes column use the
  chamfered head, and the false "in the product's order" docstring is corrected. `bench/end_cuts.py`
  imports both helpers (no new import boundary: the benches already import `screw.solid.bolt` and
  `screw.calc.thread`), passes the chamfered head at its three call sites, and its `BoltCase.closed()`
  carries the chamfer term.
- Tests that wanted the sharp part now say `bolt.head_prism(p)`.
- A probe run (macOS arm64, single runs) gave whole-part volume errors of -1.6e-6, -1.9e-8 and
  -1.2e-7 for M2 x 4, M6 x 20 and M6 x 30, and a tip-bolt piece error of 5.5e-5, so the closed
  forms agree with the chamfered parts.
- The tables in `bench/RESULTS.md` predate this and are the sharp-head probe. The two module
  docstrings now say so. They need a re-run before Phase 7 reads them; I did not re-run or edit
  them.
